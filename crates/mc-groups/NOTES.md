# mc-groups: notes (round 31)

Group theory and combinatorics, roadmap section 3.4 track G. Everything is native Rust (`src/`); there is no
library source. Decisions are numbered g31.N.

## Representation and naming

- g31.1 A permutation is the term `cycles([[1, 2, 3], [4, 5]])`: disjoint cycles of 1-based points. The native
  `cycles` puts it in canonical form (each cycle rotated to start at its least point, cycles ordered by that point,
  fixed points dropped), so equal permutations are equal terms; overlapping cycles leave the call unevaluated. The
  identity is `cycles([])`. The degree of a permutation is not part of it (functions that need one take it as an
  argument: `permutation_list(p, n)`, `permutation_cycle_type(p, n)`, `group_orbits(G, n)`, `is_transitive(G, n)`).
- g31.2 Products act on the right: `permutation_product(p, q)` applies p first, x^(pq) = (x^p)^q, and conjugation
  is a^g = g^-1 a g (`conjugating_element(G, a, b)` returns g with g^-1 a g = b). This is the convention of the
  computational group theory literature (Seress; Holt, Eick and O'Brien) and of the cycle notation read left to
  right.
- g31.3 A permutation group is `permutation_group([g1, g2, ...])`; it acts on 1..n with n its degree (the largest
  moved point). Subgroups that algorithms return carry a minimal set of generators (each one not in the group
  generated by the earlier ones). A finitely presented group is `finitely_presented_group([a, b], [relators])`:
  generators are symbols, a word is a list of letters `a`, `a^-1`, `a^k`, and nested lists concatenate, so
  `table([a, b], 5)` is (ab)^5; `u -> v` is the relation u = v. (`[a, b]^5` is not (ab)^5: powers of lists are
  elementwise in the language.)
- g31.4 Names follow the language's style: `group_order`, `group_stabilizer`, `group_centralizer`, `is_solvable`,
  `is_abelian_group` and `is_simple_group` (the plain `is_abelian` and `is_simple` would read as predicates on any
  expression), `abelian_invariants` (prime powers, then 0 for each infinite cyclic factor, for permutation and
  finitely presented groups alike). Posets are `poset(elements, relations)` with relations as pairs [a, b] (a <=
  b, closed reflexively and transitively) or a function `(a, b) => ...`; matroids are `linear_matroid(A)` (the
  columns of a rational matrix), `graphic_matroid(edges)`, `uniform_matroid(r, m)` or `matroid(bases[, m])`,
  with elements 1..m.

## Algorithms and literature

- Stabiliser chains: the deterministic incremental Schreier-Sims algorithm of Knuth ("Efficient representation of
  perm groups", Combinatorica 11, 1991; Seress, *Permutation Group Algorithms*, 4.2): every pair (orbit point,
  generator) gives one Schreier generator, sifted into the next level. Explicit transversals. Chains are cached
  by generator list (256 entries). Orders past 2^128 are kept as limbs (`group_order(symmetric_group(30))`).
- Orbits by breadth-first search; pointwise stabilisers by a chain whose base starts with the points; normal
  closure by conjugating generators until closed (Seress 2.3.3); derived and lower central series as normal
  closures of commutators of generators; cosets by breadth-first search with canonical coset representatives
  (the element of H g whose base images are least, level by level).
- Blocks: the minimal block containing {a, b} by Atkinson's union-find algorithm (Seress 5.5); a group is primitive
  when it is transitive and every minimal block containing 1 and another point is everything.
- Backtrack search (Seress 9.1; Butler, *Fundamental Algorithms for Permutation Groups*, ch. 10) for centralisers,
  set stabilisers, intersections and conjugating elements: the base is laid out along the cycles of the element
  (or the set first), a partial map of base points is pruned when cycle lengths differ or a forced image b_m =
  a(b_l) -> c_m = b(c_l) fails, and the subgroup search tries at each level only the least image in each orbit of
  the part of the subgroup already found.
- Composition series (g31.5): the derived series cuts the group into abelian layers, refined by the cyclic steps
  N < <N, g_1> < <N, g_1, g_2> < ... and those by primes through powers; a perfect layer is split at a maximal
  normal subgroup built from the normal closures of the conjugacy class representatives (every normal subgroup is
  a join of these, and adding closures while the join stays proper ends at a maximal one).
- Conjugacy classes (g31.6): up to 2,000,000 elements every element is numbered by its chain coordinates and the
  classes are the orbits of conjugation by the generators. Larger groups: random elements and their powers, a
  conjugacy test against the known representatives (backtrack), and class sizes |G|/|C(g)| from backtrack
  centralisers, until the sizes add up to |G| (Las Vegas: the answer is exact when given; M24's 26 classes take a
  few seconds). Classes are ordered by element order, then size, then cycle type.
- Character tables (g31.7): Dixon-Schneider (Dixon, Numer. Math. 10, 1967; Schneider, J. Symbolic Comput. 9,
  1990) over F_p with p = 1 mod the exponent and p > 2 sqrt|G|: common eigenvectors of the class matrices
  (c_jk^l counted by running over C_j), degrees from sum_k w_k w_k' / |C_k|, values lifted from the reductions on
  the powers of each class representative as sum_s m_s zeta_o^s. A value is an integer when the m_s are constant on
  the classes of (Z/o)^* (then it is sum_d M_d mu(o/d), by Ramanujan sums); otherwise it is the exact sum of
  powers of e^(2 pi i/o), evaluated and expanded by the engine. Rows by degree. Needs the enumerated classes (at
  most 2,000,000 elements).
- Coset enumeration: HLT with Holt's coincidence procedure (Holt, Eick and O'Brien, *Handbook of Computational
  Group Theory*, 5.1-5.2), standardised tables (Sims, *Computation with Finitely Presented Groups*, 4.7), at most
  4,000,000 live cosets. `group_order` of a presentation whose abelian invariants contain 0 is `inf` without
  enumerating.
- Knuth-Bendix completion for shortlex with a < a^-1 < b < b^-1 < ... (Sims ch. 2-3): critical pairs from
  overlaps, interreduction on every new rule, at most 2000 rules.
- Abelian invariants of a presentation: the Smith normal form of the exponent-sum matrix (checked i128
  arithmetic); of a permutation group: |G : G' G^(p^k)| for each prime p of |G/G'|.
- Posets (Stanley, *Enumerative Combinatorics* I, ch. 3): Moebius function by its recursion along a linear order;
  linear extensions listed by choosing minimal elements, counted over down-sets with memoisation (26 elements).
- Matroids (Oxley, *Matroid Theory*): a rank function (fraction-free elimination for linear matroids, union-find for
  graphic ones); bases, circuits and the Tutte polynomial by the corank-nullity sum over all subsets (ground sets
  of at most 22 elements).
- Tableaux (Sagan, *The Symmetric Group*, ch. 3; Stanley, *Enumerative Combinatorics* II, 7.11-7.13): RSK row
  insertion (words with repeated letters give a semistandard P), its inverse by reverse bumping, hook lengths, the
  hook length formula evaluated exactly by prime exponents.
- Polya counting (Polya 1937; de Bruijn, "Polya's theory of counting", 1964): the cycle index from the cycle types
  of the elements; for the full symmetric and alternating groups (recognised by the order n! or n!/2 on n points)
  from the partitions, n!/z_lambda each; else by enumerating up to 2,000,000 elements or from the conjugacy classes.
  `polya_count(G, k)` substitutes x(i) -> k, `polya_count(G, [w1, ...])` substitutes x(i) -> sum w_j^i.

## Gaps

- Character tables and the composition series of a perfect group need the classes: enumeration up to 2,000,000
  elements, or the random method (which may give up on groups with very small classes, e.g. S_n for large n; the
  composition series of S_n and A_n do not need it).
- No low-index subgroups, no normaliser, no Sylow subgroups, no presentations of permutation groups, no sporadic
  groups beyond the Mathieu groups.
- Matroid functions enumerate subsets (22 elements); no deletion-contraction for large sparse graphs.
